Planning & design

The visual choices and page structures behind First Aid, First.

Design concept

The site uses a clear magazine-style layout with a warm cream canvas, a white navigation bar, and a deep navy hero. Matching illustrations make the topic recognizable at a glance. Large headings, generous spacing, and grouped cards help visitors scan a guide before reading the details. Coral marks key actions and emergency information; aqua supports calm learning sections.

Heading type: Lexend. Body type: Source Sans 3. Both are loaded through a Google Fonts stylesheet, with Arial and sans-serif fallbacks. Lexend gives headings a clear, distinctive shape; Source Sans 3 keeps longer guides comfortable to read. Bold display headings introduce each page; body text uses a generous line height and limited reading width. Dark navy text sits on cream or white, dark teal labels sit on light sections, and action buttons use dark coral with white text.

Planning decisions

  • Audience: people seeking a quick introduction to everyday first aid.
  • Content: three focused lessons, supported by recognized health organizations.
  • Navigation: a shared white header provides the seven page links and shows the current page. Compact rounded links and wrapped navigation keep the navigation easy to use at smaller widths. Each long guide has an on-page section menu.
  • Interaction: visitors can choose a situation, try three first aid questions with feedback, and validate registration details. Visible hover and keyboard focus states identify controls.
  • Responsive layout: the large illustrated hero uses two columns on desktop and stacks on mobile. Topic cards, form fields, and the supply table adapt to the available width. Reading cards keep line lengths comfortable.

How the design helps visitors

Find a topic quickly

A topic directory links directly to CPR, choking, wounds, shock, and burn care. Clear section headings make the longer guides easy to navigate.

Read with less effort

Numbered steps, restrained colors, short paragraphs, and generous spacing separate first actions from supporting information.

Understand the result

Form feedback and learning answers appear beside the controls. Warnings use words and layout as well as color.

The UI-UX-Pro-Max skill was used as design guidance during the visual review. Its recommendations were evaluated against the course techniques and the needs of a first aid audience; it is not recorded as an additional AI service.
