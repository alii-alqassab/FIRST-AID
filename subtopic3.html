Heat, hot liquids and steam

Act quickly on a thermal burn

A hot pan can cause a burn; spilled tea or steam can cause a scald. Treat the affected skin promptly.

1

Stop the exposure safely

Move away from the heat source. Check your own safety before helping.

2

Cool for 20 minutes

Use cool running water as soon as possible. Cool bottled water can help if a tap is unavailable. Do not apply ice.

3

Remove tight items

Take off nearby rings, watches and loose clothing, but leave anything stuck to the skin.

4

Protect after cooling

Lay cling film over the area; do not wind it around a limb. Seek advice if the injury is concerning.

Source: NHS: Burns and scalds.

Recognize an emergency

When the burn needs urgent help

Call emergency services for a serious burn. In Bahrain call 999. Difficulty breathing after smoke exposure is an emergency.
  • Deep injury, a wide affected area, or skin that looks white, brown or black.
  • Burns affecting the face, mouth, hands, feet, joints or groin.
  • Injury extending around a limb, neck or chest.
  • A chemical or electrical cause.
  • A burn in a very young or older person, or someone with other injuries or medical conditions.

A serious burn may cause little pain. The amount of pain alone cannot show how badly the tissue is damaged. Watch the person’s breathing and responsiveness while help is coming.

Source: American Red Cross: Burns.

Different causes need different care

Chemical and electrical burns

Chemicals on the skin or eyes

Call emergency services immediately. Protect yourself with gloves if available. Carefully remove contaminated clothing; brush away dry chemical powder before rinsing. NHS guidance advises rinsing with plenty of cool or lukewarm water for about an hour. Follow the dispatcher and product safety instructions. Do not add another chemical to try to neutralize it.

Chemical burns need hospital assessment. Follow the emergency dispatcher’s instructions while help is on the way.

NHS: Acid and chemical burns.

Electricity

Do not approach until the power is off and the scene is safe. An apparently small mark can hide internal injury. Electricity can also affect breathing and the heart, so arrange urgent medical assessment and follow emergency instructions.

American Red Cross: Electrical burns.

After the immediate response

Protect healing skin

Small burns may take around two weeks to heal. Follow the clinician’s dressing advice. Avoid sticky plasters, leave blisters intact and ask a pharmacist about suitable pain relief or dry, itchy healing skin.

Skip home remedies: Butter, oils and creams do not belong on a fresh burn.

NHS: Burn care and recovery.

Common questions

What if a child is burned?

Start cooling promptly and get medical advice if concerned. Cool the injured area while keeping the child’s remaining body warm; children can become cold quickly.

Can I pull off stuck clothing?

No. Leave attached material in place and let healthcare professionals deal with it.

Should I burst a blister?

Leave it intact. Breaking the skin can increase the chance of infection. Ask a healthcare professional if you are worried about how the burn is healing.

Sources: NHS: Accidents in children; American Red Cross: Burns.
